# Plan

Eighteen months from idea to a product that owners committees pay for and
management companies tolerate. Dubai first.

## Phase 0: Validate and decide (now to month 1)

Goal: a signed pilot building and a defensible ownership structure.

- Interview 10 owners committee members and 5 management company operations heads.
  Ask what they lose at handover, what lapsed last year, and what they would pay from
  the service charge budget.
- Get a written legal opinion on the Dubai jointly owned property regime: can the
  owners association own the device and data, and can the ledger be a compliance
  record. Same question, lighter touch, for Saudi.
- Pick the pilot tower: cooperative committee, measurable manager, two years of history.
- Decide the working name and register the entity.
- Defer the hardware decision. The pilot runs on whatever box is available.

Exit: pilot agreement signed, legal opinion in hand, name chosen.

## Phase 1: Build v1 (months 1 to 3)

Goal: the document-and-schedule brain working on the pilot building's real archive.

- Ingest: OCR, classification, versioning of the pilot building's full document set.
- Obligations: extraction with citations. Reviewed line by line with the manager.
- Scheduler: PPM and statutory calendar generated from obligations.
- Delegation: email and WhatsApp assignment, chasing, escalation, evidence capture.
- Reporting: weekly committee report, automatic.
- Ledger: append-only, hash-chained, exportable.
- Dubai regulation corpus: Civil Defence, RERA and Mollak, DEWA, Dubai Municipality.
  One researcher, full time, with a review process.

Team: founder, two engineers, one regulatory researcher, one part-time FM advisor who
has run towers in Dubai.

Exit: the brain runs the pilot building's calendar for two weeks in shadow mode and
the manager agrees it is right.

## Phase 2: Pilot (months 4 to 6)

Goal: evidence.

- Brain goes live. Manager confirms tier 3 items. Committee gets weekly reports.
- Track the measures in PILOT.md: certificates lapsed, PPM on time, contractor
  response, obligations nobody was tracking, committee hours saved.
- Interview the manager and committee monthly. Record what the brain got wrong.

Exit: committee would pay for a second quarter, manager would recommend to a peer.

## Phase 3: Productise (months 7 to 9)

Goal: a thing that can be sold and handed over.

- Run a simulated change of management on the pilot building. Revoke, seal, provision.
  Fix whatever breaks.
- Choose and spec the device: enclosure, UPS, attestation, provisioning image.
- Pricing: device plus annual licence, paid from service charge, approved by committee.
- Sign buildings two and three, ideally under different management companies.
- Start Abu Dhabi and Saudi regulation corpora.

Exit: three buildings live, handover protocol proven, price list published.

## Phase 4: Expand (months 10 to 18)

Goal: repeatable sales and the telemetry layer.

- Channel: developers at handover, and management companies who want to differentiate.
- Telemetry: start with CCTV health and retrofit sensors on fire pumps and generators.
  Lift OEM integration where the OEM allows it.
- Saudi launch with a local partner.
- Raise on pilot evidence, not on the vision.

Exit: ten buildings, two jurisdictions, one channel partner, telemetry live in at least
three buildings.

## Risks and the answer to each

| Risk | Answer |
|------|--------|
| Regulators reject AI-generated compliance records | Human sign-off on every tier 3 item; ledger is evidence, the human is the record |
| Management companies block adoption | Sell through committees and developers; offer managers legibility, not replacement |
| Model asserts a wrong regulation | Cite-or-silence rule; curated corpus; reviewed updates |
| Integration hell | Phase telemetry last; obligations never depend on a sensor |
| Device dies in a plant room | Rated enclosure, UPS, nightly encrypted snapshot to committee-held storage |
| Founder runs out of money before evidence | Phase 2 is the only thing that matters; everything else is cut to reach it |
